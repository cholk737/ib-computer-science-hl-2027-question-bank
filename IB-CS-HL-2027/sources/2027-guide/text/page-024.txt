Approaches to learning and approaches to teaching in computer science




Skill category                   Examples of skills development in the classroom

Communication skills            •      Practise active listening skills.
                                •      Reflect on the needs of the audience when translating technical concepts
                                       into general understandings.
                                •      Develop skills in written and diagrammatic communication relevant to
                                       computer science (e.g. system overview diagrams, flowcharts, UML
                                       diagrams).
                                •      Communicate solutions with other computer scientists.
                                •      Use terminology, symbols and programming styles consistently and
                                       correctly.
                                •      Present data appropriately to recognize data patterns.
                                •      Communicate constructive criticism appropriately when evaluating the
                                       solutions of other computer scientists.
                                •      Use effective comments and correct syntax.
                                •      Use efficient computational instructions and conventions to increase
                                       readability and aid debugging.

Social skills                   •      Recognize the importance of collaboration in computer science (e.g. open-
                                       source movements).
                                •      Work in teams to achieve common goals in different types of collaborative
                                       environments.
                                •      Assign and accept specific roles during collaborative activities, recognizing
                                       their significance in the process of computational thinking.
                                •      Appreciate the diverse talents and needs of others.
                                •      Resolve conflicts during collaborative work.
                                •      Actively seek and consider the perspective of others.
                                •      Constructively assess the contribution of peers.
Research skills                 •      Use human, printed and digital resources effectively.
                                •      Evaluate information sources for accuracy, bias, credibility and relevance.
                                •      Discuss the importance of academic integrity and acknowledge the ideas
                                       of others.
                                •      Use a consistent, standard method of referencing and citation.
                                •      Compare, contrast and validate information.

Self-management skills          •      Develop organizational skills through goal setting and planning.
                                •      Manage tasks with effective time management.
                                •      Break down major tasks into a sequence of stages.
                                •      Be punctual and meet deadlines.
                                •      Regard setbacks as part of the process towards solutions.
                                •      Set learning goals and adjust them in response to experience.
                                •      Seek and act on feedback.

Approaches to teaching

The teaching of IB programmes, based on a constructivist and student-centred approach, models its
essential pedagogical structure on six principles.
•     Inquiry that strengthens creativity, autonomy, communication skills and organizational skills
